# G2.1 — Storage tank energy balance (guided)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Learning objective** | Write the energy balance of each node of an open system with a through-flow and verify that the tank as a whole conserves energy (session outcomes 1 and 3). |
| **Prerequisites** | Lesson §5 (open-system energy balance, M03 node equations). |
| **Starting files** | `exercises/session_02/s02_g1_storage.py` |
| **Expected duration** | 15 min |
| **Expected outcome** | Node equations that agree with `energy_training.simulation.storage.rhs`; energy-balance residual below $10^{-4}$ of the energy throughput; times at which the outlet reaches 50 °C for N = 1, 5 and 20, and a numerical check of the lesson's statement about what N does and does not change. |

Parameters for this exercise (illustrative): tank $V$ = 200 L, $N$ = 5 nodes, $UA_{loss}$ = 1.5 W/K,
no conduction between nodes, water properties $\rho$ = 995.586 kg/m³, $c_p$ = 4184 J/(kg·K).
The tank starts uniformly at 25 °C; from $t = 0$ water at 55 °C enters at the top with
$\dot m$ = 0.05 kg/s and leaves at the bottom; surroundings at 20 °C. Simulate 3 h.

## Instructions

1. By hand: mass and heat capacity of one node, and the mean residence time $\rho V/\dot m$.
2. Complete `rhs()` in the starting file: the energy balance of each node (node 1 at the top).
3. Simulate with `scipy.integrate.solve_ivp` (`rtol = atol = 1e-8`), output every 60 s.
4. Compute the three energy terms over the 3 h — stored, brought in by the flow, lost through the
   insulation — and the residual. Is the residual below $10^{-4}$ of the throughput?
5. Check your `rhs()` against `energy_training.simulation.storage.rhs` at a few random states.
6. Before running: the lesson claims that $N$ changes the *shape* of the outlet front but not the
   mean residence time. Predict whether the outlet reaches 50 °C sooner or later with more nodes.
   Then repeat the simulation with $N$ = 1 and $N$ = 20, find when the outlet reaches 50 °C, and
   check both the claim and your prediction.

## Extension activity

Add conduction between neighbouring nodes ($G$ = 2 W/K). Show that the energy balance of the
tank does not change, and explain why the conduction terms cancel in the sum over the nodes.
