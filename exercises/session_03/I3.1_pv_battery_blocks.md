# I3.1 — Simplified PV and battery blocks

| | |
|---|---|
| **Learning objective** | write `block`s with `RealInput`/`RealOutput`, a state with `der()` and an `if`-expression, and drive them from a time table |
| **Prerequisites** | Session 3 §4.4 (classes), §5.4 (PV and battery equations) |
| **Starting files** | `exercises/session_03/S03_I1_PVBattery_start.mo` |
| **Expected duration** | 10 min in class for steps 1–2, the rest at home (+ extension) |
| **Expected outcome** | state of charge over one day and a closed energy balance: energy sent to the battery = change of stored energy + battery losses |

## Instructions

The package contains two blocks to complete and an example that uses them. The irradiance table
is the global horizontal irradiance of 15 April from the course's Turin typical year (Session 0
data), hourly, linearly interpolated. Sizes are illustrative: 15 m² of panel, 300 W of load, a
5 kWh battery starting at $SOC$ = 0.5.

1. `SimplePV`: declare the input `G` [W/m²] (the irradiance on the panel, here $G_h$) and the output
   `P` [W], and write $P = A\,f_{act}\,\eta\,G_h$.
2. `SimpleBattery`: declare the output $SOC$, its initial value $SOC_{start}$, and the energy
   balance $E_{max}\,\dot{SOC} = P_{act}$ with $P_{act} = \eta_{cha}P$ when $P > 0$ (charging) and
   $P/\eta_{dis}$ otherwise.
3. `Example`: the energies are already declared ($E_{pv} = \int P_{pv}\,dt$, $E_{load}$, the change
   of stored energy $E_{bat} = E_{max}(SOC - SOC_{start})$ and the losses
   $E_{loss} = E_{pv} - E_{load} - E_{bat}$). Complete the connections.
4. Simulate one day. Report $SOC$ at the end, its minimum and maximum, and the four energies in kWh.
   Is $E_{loss}$ ever negative? Why must it grow only while power flows through the battery?
5. Why are `SimplePV` and `SimpleBattery` declared as `block` and not as `model`? What would
   break if you added a `HeatPort` to `SimpleBattery`? (In Session 5 the Buildings battery is a
   `model` with an electrical connector, and its default $SOC_{start}$ is 0.1.)

## Extension activity

Change the table to 21 March (`load_tmy().loc["2023-03-21":"2023-03-22", "G_h"].iloc[:25]` in
Python). The state of charge exceeds 1. Nothing in the model prevents it — what would? In Session 5
the same equations come from the Buildings library, which warns when $SOC$ leaves [0, 1] but does
not stop it; Sessions 4–5 add events and an operating rule.
