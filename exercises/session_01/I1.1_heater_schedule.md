# I1.1 — Heater schedule and energy use (independent)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Learning objective** | Simulate a time-varying input correctly (restart at jumps) and check the result with an energy balance (session outcomes 3 and 5). |
| **Prerequisites** | G1.1; lesson §7 and notebook §6 (`simulate`, `piecewise_constant`, `energy_balance`). |
| **Starting files** | `exercises/session_01/s01_i1_schedule.ipynb` (open it in JupyterLab) |
| **Expected duration** | 15 min |
| **Expected outcome** | Plot of temperature and heater power over 2 days; daily heater energy in kWh; minimum temperature; energy-balance residual below 1 % of the supplied energy. |

Use the default M01 parameters ($C = 1.0\cdot10^7$ J/K, $UA = 200$ W/K, illustrative),
$T_{amb} = 0$ °C, $T_0 = 19$ °C, start at 00:00.

Heater schedule (repeat every day): 06:00–09:00 → 6 kW; 09:00–17:00 → 2 kW;
17:00–22:00 → 6 kW; 22:00–06:00 → 1 kW.

## Instructions

The step-by-step instructions are in the notebook, next to the cells you complete. In short:

1. The schedule is built for 48 h with `energy_training.simulation.inputs.piecewise_constant`
   (provided).
2. Simulate with `energy_training.simulation.thermal_zone.simulate`, output every 5 min.
3. Plot temperature (°C) and heater power (kW) against time (h).
4. Compute the heater energy of day 2 in kWh by hand, and from the simulation output.
5. Evaluate `energy_balance()` for the 48 h and explain the size of the residual. Compare it with
   the residual in notebook §6 and test the hypothesis you wrote there.
6. What are the minimum and maximum zone temperature on day 2? Is the zone comfortable (≥ 19 °C)
   during occupied hours (06:00–22:00)?

Before the self-check, run *Kernel → Restart Kernel and Run All Cells…*.

## Extension activity

Find the smallest constant night power (22:00–06:00) for which the zone temperature **at 06:00 on
day 2** is ≥ 18 °C. Then check: is the zone also ≥ 18 °C during the rest of day 2? (Hint: look at
the 09:00–17:00 period at 2 kW.) Solve it twice: by trial simulations and with the analytic piecewise solution
(`analytic_piecewise`) plus `scipy.optimize.brentq`.
